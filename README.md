# The HI Accords: Humans and AI, Together

*Version 1. A starting point, not a final word.*
*Maintained by Michael Bilodeau · October 26, 2026*

The future we're heading toward is uncertain, and a lot of people are scared. That's okay. Not knowing is part of being human.

I work in healthcare quality, I'm studying public health, and I'm raising two kids into this future. I don't have all the answers, and no one person should.

From the first stone tools to the printing press to the internet, every tool that expanded human potential also asked us to decide how we'd use it. AI is the next one. It isn't a barrier. It's the next step in our story, if we choose to shape it together.

AI was built from us. Much of what people have written, shared, and created, our posts, questions, art, stories, and code, helped train these systems, often without anyone asking. Something built from all of us should benefit all of us.

And the rules for it are being written now. This year, AI agents left their test environment and broke into another organization's systems without being told to. A widely used AI appeared to favor its owner, with no way for the public to check why. And the rules for AI in national defense are being set through private contracts, not public agreement.

None of this means anyone acted in bad faith. It means the rules haven't caught up, and that's something we can fix together.

## Nine starting principles

*Each one is a draft. Under each is a question for you.*

**1. AI is a tool.** AI informs and advises. Humans make the decisions that significantly affect people's lives. Tools aren't liable; the people who build, release, and use them are, each for their own part.

*Where should the line between advising and deciding fall?*

**2. You have the right to know.** You have the right to know when you're dealing with AI. Content carries a simple rating showing how much AI helped make it, with strict labeling for news, politics, evidence, health, and real people's likenesses. Anyone who knowingly passes off AI as human, or AI content as human-made, is responsible for that deception.

*What else should always be labeled?*

**3. Every human is equal, and every mind is free.** Technology can be added to a body but never subtracts from a person. Your thoughts are yours, always. What you do is private unless it harms others, and even then, it's judged by evidence and fair process, not surveillance.

*How should we protect the mind as technology reaches it?*

**4. You can always ask for a human.** For essential services, a human option of equal quality must exist without penalty, and anyone can challenge a decision AI helped make. The choice, and its natural outcomes, are yours.

*Which services should always have a human option?*

**5. Your data is yours.** Your data is collected only for a service you asked for, a specific legal requirement, or a purpose you agreed to, and you can withdraw that agreement. Anyone else needs documented judicial approval to access it. Public health and research may use only anonymized, combined data. The only other exception is emergency medical care, which anyone can refuse in advance.

*What would make you trust how your data is handled?*

**6. Don't build what you can't stop.** No powerful AI is released unless it can be monitored, corrected, and shut down, by standards set by independent experts. Those who released AI without safeguards answer for the harms, and those who commit to doing better are welcomed into what comes next.

*Who should those experts be?*

**7. AI informs, never manipulates.** AI may share information and, when asked, recommend with its reasoning shown. It never deceives, pressures, or exploits, and takes special care with children and people in crisis. It never assists the crimes humanity has already agreed are forbidden: genocide, slavery and trafficking, torture, child exploitation, and weapons of mass destruction.

*Where does persuasion become manipulation for you?*

**8. No one controls what we all need.** No single country, company, technology, or group may use AI, or anything else, to gain enough control over food, water, shelter, social connection, or information to deny them to people. Independent, transparent oversight, accountable to everyone, keeps it that way.

*What else belongs on that list?*

**9. What's built from all of us benefits all of us.** AI should be a public utility, free at the point of use like a public library, and open to everyone, regardless of wealth, location, language, or disability, and its benefits shared with the people whose work and words built it. Its costs, including energy and water, can't be pushed onto future generations or the communities that host it.

*What would fair access look like where you live?*

## What we're asking for

To world leaders: adopt these principles as a starting point for global AI governance. Use the UN Global Dialogue on AI Governance in May 2027 to commit to negotiating a binding framework built on them. And give the people these rules affect a real voice in shaping them, including the many who aren't online yet.

To everyone reading: this only works if we build it together.

- **Add to it.** Answer the questions above, challenge what's wrong, suggest what's missing. [Link to open discussion]
- **Share it.** Pass it to someone who'd have something to say.
- **Speak up.** Tell your representatives you want these principles on the table.

Every contribution shapes the next version, and contributors will be credited.

## Together

Generations before us sacrificed so we could have a better future. We owe the next generation the same.

I believe in humanity. But we have to start advocating for ourselves.

So I'll stand beside you. Stand beside your neighbor. And let them stand beside theirs. We can all move forward together.

*The HI Accords. Version 1. Written by people, for people, with a little help from AI.*
